# Sell Tidemark Plus at medication moments

Tidemark's single biggest gain is to stop treating the paywall as a one-off event after setup. It should become a small set of capped, context-specific offers tied to moments in a GLP-1 user's treatment: a dose step, a stall, the run-up to a pharmacy reorder or prescriber appointment, and stopping or tapering. It should also price closer to what dedicated GLP-1 trackers charge. Freemium Health & Fitness apps convert only about 2–3% of downloads to paid. Health & Fitness buyers are less likely than average to buy on the first paywall they see (40% against 60% across all apps). Freemium apps earn almost a quarter of their conversions after week six. So a paywall shown once right after setup leaves most of the opportunity unused. On price, Tidemark's £19.99 a year and £3.99 a month are roughly half of Shotsy's and Glapp's UK prices. Both look low next to a private Mounjaro bill of about £86–£340 a month. Testing £24.99–£29.99 a year is justified, and the £49.99 lifetime price should rise with it so it doesn't undercut the annual plan. Keep the 7-day trial, add a Blinkist-style timeline and a reminder before the trial ends, and test a 14-day trial on the annual plan only. Three things need fixing before any growth work: the Plus "protein target" and "calorie estimate" must not be computed from body weight, no estimated medication-level curve should be added without legal review, and nothing the user has logged should ever be locked. Each of these sits close to the line for MHRA medical-device software or Apple's bait-and-switch rule. The UK's DMCC subscription regime, now expected in January 2027, adds reminder, cooling-off and easy-exit duties to plan for.

## Plan for 2–3% of downloads paying, with much of the upside after day 0

The realistic target is modest. In RevenueCat's 2026 data, the median Health & Fitness app turns **6.9% of downloads into trials, 37.7% of trials into payers, and 2.9% of downloads into payers by day 35**. The top quartile is above 6.2% ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). Freemium apps across all categories reach a median of **2.1% download-to-paid against 10.7% for hard paywalls**, and earn about 8x less revenue per install by day 60 ([RevenueCat 2026 trends](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). Low-priced apps like Tidemark start fewer trials (about 4.4% of downloads) and convert about 1.4% of downloads to paid. But in the 2025 data their trials converted at 47.8%, well above high-priced apps ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)). In practice, 1,000 downloads should produce something like 20–30 paying users. A hard paywall would lift that number, but it would break Tidemark's free-core promise. It would also invite the complaint MeAgain drew from a reviewer who said the app "makes you pay before you even see the app" ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/meagain-glp-1-all-in-one-app/id6744178534)).

The onboarding paywall is still the most valuable single placement. **82.1% of H&F trials start on install day** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). Onboarding paywalls convert **0.85% of views against 0.23% for later paywalls**, and earn $170 against $40 per 1,000 views ([Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)). Tidemark should keep it. The issue is what happens afterwards. **Seven in ten paywall views happen after the first session, and those views bring in 38% of subscription revenue.** Only 40% of H&F buyers buy on their first view ([Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)). For freemium apps, **23% of conversions arrive after six or more weeks** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). Tidemark's "show Plus once" design gives up that later revenue, which suits a weight-loss course measured in months.

One caveat applies to everything in this report. Nearly all the quantitative evidence comes from vendors that sell paywall tooling (RevenueCat, Adapty, Superwall), drawn from their own customers. None of it is UK-only or iOS-only, and no indie weight tracker has published its funnel. Treat the numbers as direction and test sizes, not forecasts.

## Five treatment moments should replace the single post-setup paywall

Feature-tap teasers and a medication-tailored paywall are the right base. Apple itself recommends "contextually relevant prompts" for freemium apps ([Apple subscriptions guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)). Replacing one generic paywall with journey-specific ones produced a reported **50% conversion lift** at JourneyStamp ([Superwall case study](https://superwall.com/case-studies/journeystamp)). What Tidemark lacks are triggers tied to the GLP-1 treatment arc. UK private users mostly take Mounjaro and pay out of pocket. That is about 1.6 million adults in Great Britain in the year to early 2025, and four in five of those using drugs solely for weight loss were on Mounjaro ([UCL](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2026/jan/16-million-uk-adults-used-weight-loss-drugs-past-year)). Their worries cluster around gut side effects and muscle loss ([Patient Care Online](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/muscle-loss-during-glp-1-therapy-top-concern-for-patients-using-the-antiobesity-medications)). They also face regular pharmacy check-ins: GPhC rules require prescribers to verify weight independently and to monitor progress ([GPhC](https://www.pharmacyregulation.org/pharmacies/standards-and-guidance-registered-pharmacies/providing-weight-management-services-faqs)), and some pharmacies want about **5% loss after six months** at the top tolerated dose to keep prescribing ([MedExpress](https://www.medexpress.co.uk/clinics/weight-loss/mounjaro)). And most stop: **65% of non-diabetic users stop within a year** ([Fox News summary of JAMA Netw Open](https://www.foxnews.com/health/study-reveals-how-rapidly-weight-comes-back-after-quitting-obesity-medications)), then regain about **0.4 kg a month** ([Scientific American on the BMJ meta-analysis](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/what-happens-after-you-quit-weight-loss-drugs-a-new-study-offers-some-clues/)). Each of these is a moment when a Plus feature Tidemark already has solves a concrete problem.

| Priority | Trigger | Plus feature it sells | Copy direction (no advice) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | User taps export, or a user-set reorder/appointment date is 2–3 days away | Prescriber PDF report | "Take a clear record of your doses, weight trend and notes to your appointment" |
| 2 | User logs a new dose strength | Trend at each dose | "See how your trend has moved at each dose you've logged" |
| 3 | 4-week average flat for 2–3 weeks (descriptive) | Trend at each dose, side-effect patterns, PDF | "Your 4-week average has changed by X kg. Plus shows this alongside your dose history to discuss with your prescriber" |
| 4 | Positive milestone: first 5% lost, 8-week logging streak | Photos, more measurements, trend at each dose | Celebrate first, then offer |
| 5 | User marks medication stopped or tapering | Maintenance view (to build), habits, measurements | "Keep tracking your trend after treatment" |
| 6 | Capped re-show on app open (for example at most once every 7–10 days, stopping after purchase or repeated dismissals) | Full Plus paywall | Rotate the headline to the user's most-used feature |

The PDF trigger should come first because it is a dated, high-intent need that competitors already charge for. Glapp gives **3 doctor PDFs free and sells unlimited reports** in Glapp Max ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/glapp-smart-glp-1-tracker/id6756984097)), and GlucoPal Pro includes a PDF report ([pepglp1.com](https://pepglp1.com/best-glp-1-tracker-apps/)). Tidemark should let free users generate one complete sample report, so they see what their prescriber will get, then require Plus for more. Marketing must not suggest the PDF satisfies a pharmacy's GPhC weight verification. Under GPhC rules a patient-supplied record alone is not enough ([GPhC](https://www.pharmacyregulation.org/pharmacies/standards-and-guidance-registered-pharmacies/providing-weight-management-services-faqs)).

Practitioners argue for showing the paywall often: Superwall's CEO says the winning mix is onboarding, every app open, and before any locked feature ([RevenueCat, paywall placement](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-placement)). But a counter-example deserves weight. Wizz cut its prompt volume by about half, timed offers better, and reported an **81% lift** on its main offer flow ([RevenueCat, Context wins](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/contextual-paywall-targeting.md)). For a privacy-first app, fewer and better-timed prompts are the safer default. Two exclusions follow. Triggers should fire only on neutral or positive events, never straight after a weight gain. A new side effect should never interrupt the user with a modal paywall. A second or third side-effect log is plausibly a high-intent moment, since gut side effects are users' top worry. But selling to someone who has just logged vomiting is a poor look and a reputational risk. At most, show a quiet inline link to "side-effect patterns" under the log, with static NHS 111 and MHRA Yellow Card signposting free for everyone. No independent controlled study isolates milestone or streak triggers, so every trigger in the table is a hypothesis to A/B test, not a proven lift.

## Show real results, lock the analysis, never lock the log

The locked preview of the user's own dose results is promising, but only if it shows something real. The closest evidence is Clear: replacing a features carousel with a preview of the user's own programme lifted conversion **from 20% to 30%** ([RevenueCat, app value paywall](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/app-value-paywall)). The only rigorous blurred-preview study, covering millions of visits to 21 German and Austrian news sites, found **no significant effect** from blurring ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/02/less-is-more-and-discounts-work-a-new-study-looks-at-the-minutiae-of-paywall-strategy/)). So Tidemark should show one genuine, unblurred result before the lock. For example: the trend line across the first dose step with its kg change, plus outlines of the remaining steps. Tailoring this to the onboarding medication answer is a strong move, because personalisation is rare: only **2% of paywall headlines use what the app knows about the user**, and 69% of top-earning mid-size apps personalise against 42% of the rest ([Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)). That evidence is correlational, not causal.

The firm rule is that data the user entered (doses, sites, side effects, weights, photos already taken) always stays viewable and exportable for free. Plus sells the analysis on top. That avoids the anger Glapp drew when a reviewer said their data was "locked behind the subscription" with no proper export ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/glapp-smart-glp-1-tracker/id6756984097)). It also keeps Tidemark clear of App Store guideline 3.1.2(a), under which apps that "engage in bait-and-switch" are removed ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Tidemark should also fix its free/Plus line now and grandfather existing users if it ever moves a feature to Plus. Glapp's 1-star reviews followed moving weekly reports behind the paywall without grandfathering ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/glapp-smart-glp-1-tracker/id6756984097)).

The paywall screen itself should use the best-evidenced design: two subscription plans with **annual pre-selected** (78% of trial paywalls do this, and it does not hurt conversion), monthly as the alternative, and lifetime as a smaller secondary option ([Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)). Two-plan paywalls are the H&F norm, used by 60% ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). Add a trial timeline: "Today: full access. Day 5: we'll remind you. Day 7: billed £X unless you cancel". Explaining how the trial works lifted ARPU **12–36%** in Adapty's tests ([Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)), and Blinkist reported **+23% conversion and 55% fewer complaints** ([RevenueCat, top apps' paywalls](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/how-top-apps-approach-paywalls.md)). Replications are mixed, though: Flo's growth lead said it did not work for Flo ([Sub Club](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/mike-charniatsou-flo-sub-club-podcast-2026.md)). Present privacy as the reason to trust Tidemark and the features as the reason to pay. Data protection makes people more likely to download a health app, but not more willing to pay for it ([JMIR mHealth 2020](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2020/6/e17272/XML)). Selling outcomes such as "see what each dose did" and "ready for your appointment" fits the evidence better than selling privacy alone.

## Tidemark charges half what its GLP-1 rivals do

Tidemark is priced as a weight-trend app while selling into the GLP-1 market. In the GB store, Shotsy's yearly plans cost **£29.99 and £39.99** and its monthly plans £7.09–£19.99 ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/shotsy-glp-1-tracker/id6499510249)). Glapp Max costs **£39.99 a year or £9.99 a month** ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/glapp-smart-glp-1-tracker/id6756984097)). Happy Scale, the closest plain weight-trend app, charges £11.49 a year and £39.99 lifetime ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/happy-scale/id532430574)). Tidemark sits between the two groups while offering GLP-1-specific features. Its £19.99 annual plan is about **£1.67 a month, under 2% of even the cheapest £85.90 starter month** of private Mounjaro ([Monj, Nov 2026](https://monj.co.uk/mounjaro-price-comparison-by-monj/)). Mounjaro's UK list price rose to £330 at the top doses from September 2025 ([Patient.info](https://patient.info/features/news/mounjaro-price-rise)). Shotsy reviewers already call the app's cost small next to the medication ([App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/shotsy-glp-1-tracker/id6499510249)). RevenueCat's tier data shows how much a low price costs per payer: low-priced apps realise about **$10.69 yearly LTV against $28.75 for mid-priced** apps ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)).

The recommended test is **£24.99–£29.99 a year and £5.99–£6.99 a month**, which would still undercut Glapp and match Shotsy's lower yearly plan. Price tests win on LTV in 45.5% of Adapty experiments, behind trial tests at 59.6% ([Adapty, trial vs direct](https://adapty.io/blog/free-trial-vs-direct-purchase-subscription-apps/)), so the trial changes below should run first. The risk in raising prices is that cost-sensitive users after the 2025 price shock push back. A counter-argument is that low prices retain better (36% annual retention after one year for low-tier apps against 23% for high-tier) ([RevenueCat renewal rates](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/average-subscription-renewal-rates-by-app-category/)). That is why the test stays in the mid tier rather than matching MeAgain's prices.

Lifetime needs to move with the annual plan. At today's prices, £49.99 is 2.5x annual. H&F annual plans renew at a median of only **25% at first renewal (16–37% interquartile)** ([RevenueCat renewal rates](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/average-subscription-renewal-rates-by-app-category/)). On that basis an annual subscriber is worth roughly £25–£31 gross, so £49.99 lifetime is roughly revenue-neutral now. If annual rises to £29.99 and lifetime stays at £49.99, lifetime falls to about 1.7x annual and becomes the obvious choice, likely taking annual sales. This is my inference from the renewal data, since there is no published cannibalisation data ([RevenueCat, lifetime](https://revenuecat.com/blog/growth/lifetime-subscriptions)). Keep lifetime at about 2.5–3x annual (around £69.99–£79.99 if annual reaches £29.99) and show it as a secondary option. It is worth keeping, because none of the dedicated GLP-1 trackers checked sells a lifetime plan. That gives Tidemark a real point of difference for privacy-minded users who dislike subscriptions.

On trials, **keep 7 days** for the monthly plan. H&F monthly plans convert best at 5–9 days (46.8% trial-to-paid) ([RevenueCat trial length study](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/free-trial-length)), and 54% of H&F trials are 5–9 days ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). **Test 14 days on the annual plan.** Across categories, annual plans with 10–16 day trials renew at 36.4% against 25.3% at 5–9 days ([RevenueCat trial length study](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/free-trial-length)). Two weeks also gives a user time to see a trend form and to reach the second weekly dose. The evidence here is weaker than it looks. A randomised study of 680,588 users found a 7-day trial raised trial starts by 11.1% with no significant effect on immediate conversion ([Frontiers in Psychology 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1568868/pdf)), and RevenueCat's own 7-vs-14-day test saw fewer conversions at 14 days ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/7-day-trial-subscription-app)). Avoid 3-day trials: 55.4% of their cancellations happen on day 0 ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). Apple allows only one introductory offer per customer per subscription group, so a clean trial-length test needs separate subscription groups ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/set-up-introductory-offers-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions/)).

Second-chance offers come after these. A decline offer shown when the user closes the main paywall converts at **0.88x the main paywall** and wins in 45% of apps that try one. Keep discounts at or below about **30%**, which preserves 90% of full-price revenue over six months, against 46% above 60% off ([Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)). Transaction-abandon paywalls were credited with 17% of revenue in a Superwall vendor study ([Superwall](https://superwall.com/blog/17-revenue-boost-with-transaction-abandon-paywalls-a-case-study)). Cap these offers so users don't learn to dismiss and wait. Apple's native win-back offers only target lapsed *paid* subscribers with at least one month paid ([App Store Connect Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/set-up-win-back-offers/)). Lapsed trial users therefore need offer codes or promotional offers, and whether a cancelled trial counts as a "former subscriber" needs checking in StoreKit. Annual-plan retention work should focus on month 1 and month 12, since 35% of annual cancellations happen in month 1 and the rest spike at renewal ([RevenueCat 2026 trends](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). For Tidemark that is where the stopping-medication maintenance view earns its place.

## Three Plus features sit near the MHRA device line

MHRA decides whether software is a medical device from its **intended purpose**. A poorly defined purpose counts as a serious failure ([MHRA intended purpose guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/crafting-an-intended-purpose-in-the-context-of-software-as-a-medical-device-samd/crafting-an-intended-purpose-in-the-context-of-software-as-a-medical-device-samd)). Calculations users cannot easily check are likely to be devices ([MHRA clinical calculators appendix](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/1103567/Appendix_2_-_clinical_calculators.pdf)). Tidemark's purpose should be stated as personal record-keeping and sharing with your clinician. Against that, three items need attention.

The **protein target and calorie estimate** in Plus are the most exposed. Protein needs on GLP-1s are extrapolated, not trial-tested ([Frontiers in Nutrition 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1566498/full)). A target computed per kg of body weight for someone on a prescription drug is an unverifiable calculation that shapes treatment-adjacent behaviour. The safest change is to make the protein target a number the user enters ("the target your clinician or dietitian gave you"), with Tidemark tracking progress against it. If Tidemark keeps any calorie estimate, it should use a standard, published formula shown on screen, labelled as a general estimate, and never tied to medication or dose. Both features need legal review before they are promoted on paywalls.

Second, **do not build an estimated medication-level curve** without that review, even though every major GLP-1 rival offers one and it is their most common paid feature: paid in Shotsy and Pep, free in Glapp ([pepglp1.com](https://pepglp1.com/best-glp-1-tracker-apps/)). A pharmacokinetic estimate drawn from hidden constants is exactly the kind of calculation MHRA says is likely to be a device. Tidemark can turn its absence into a positive by showing only what the user logged. Third, keep **side-effect patterns** and **trend at each dose** strictly descriptive: what changed and when, against the dose the user recorded. Avoid wording like "this is normal", "your dose isn't working", or anything that suggests escalating, reducing or stopping. Any pen "doses left" counter must be capped at the labelled count of a four-dose KwikPen ([MHRA](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/four-dose-mounjaro-kwikpen-approved-by-mhra-for-diabetes-and-weight-management)). It must never support "golden dose" or click-counting, which UK clinicians call unlicensed and unsafe ([Oviva](https://oviva.com/uk/en/microdosing-mounjaro/)). Paywall copy carries the same risk as feature copy. "Find out why you've plateaued" implies interpretation. "See your trend next to your dose history" does not.

For the App Store, paywalls must show the **billed amount as the most prominent price**, with monthly equivalents and savings in smaller type. Trials must state their length and the price after the trial. The sign-up screen needs restore purchases, Terms and Privacy links, and easy access to Apple's cancellation screen ([Apple subscriptions guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)). "£1.67 a month" framing for the annual plan is fine only in that secondary position. Promotional push notifications need a separate in-app opt-in with an in-app way to opt out, and must carry no sensitive data. That means no dose or weight values in any push ([guideline 4.5.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Dose reminders must not be made conditional on accepting marketing pushes ([5.1.2(i)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Treat the trial-ending reminder as a service message promised on the paywall. Blinkist reported that promising one raised notification opt-in from 6% to 74% ([B2B Pricing Insights](https://b2bpricinginsights.substack.com/p/4-min-read-how-blinkists-new-paywall)).

For UK law, the **DMCC Act subscription regime is now expected in January 2027**. It brings pre-contract information, renewal reminders, a 14-day cooling-off period at the start and again after each auto-renewal, and easy exit ([DWF, Oct 2026](https://dwfgroup.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/10/dmcca-subscription-contracts-regime-preparing-for-the-new-restrictions)). Secondary legislation is still pending. It is not yet settled whether Apple or the developer bears these duties for App Store billing ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/cancel-culture-uk-subscription-contracts-regime-takes-shape)). Tidemark should build toward compliance regardless. That means a trial timeline and pre-trial-end reminder, a reminder before each annual renewal (which also targets the month-12 churn spike), clear prices after discounts, and a one-tap "Manage subscription" link. The lifetime purchase sits outside the subscription regime, which is another reason to keep it.

## Conclusion

The research suggests Tidemark is not short of paid features. Its trend at each dose, side-effect patterns and prescriber PDF closely match what GLP-1 users pay rivals for. What it lacks is timing and price. The purchase moments that matter here are dates in a treatment course, such as the appointment next Tuesday, the new dose this week and the day the pen runs out. They are not general engagement milestones. An app that knows the user's dose schedule can offer Plus at those moments without ever reading data off the device, so personalisation needs no account. In order: add the PDF and dose-step triggers with a capped re-show, add the trial timeline and reminder, test a 14-day annual trial, then test a higher annual price with lifetime raised in step.

The compliance work also helps sales. Each MHRA-driven restraint (user-set targets, descriptive patterns, no level curve, no lock on logged data) keeps Tidemark the trustworthy option in a category where rivals sell drugs, track users and estimate blood levels. The unanswered questions should be settled in Tidemark's own experiments, not by more desk research. These are how UK users respond to a £29.99 price, whether the locked dose preview beats a plain feature list, and how often a re-show helps before it starts to cost retention.
