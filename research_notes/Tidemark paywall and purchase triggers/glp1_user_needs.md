# GLP-1 weight-loss users: needs, willingness to pay, and market size (UK focus, US secondary)

Research date: 23 November 2026. Every statistic below is dated. Several fetches of primary pages (UCL news release, PwC, Withings) returned HTTP 403, so some figures come from search-result summaries of those pages or from secondary coverage; this is noted where it applies. Many UK price figures come from pharmacy or price-comparison blogs, which are commercially interested and change often.

## 1. Market size: UK and US users, private vs NHS, prices and monthly spend

### Takeaway
About 1.6 million adults in Great Britain used weight-loss drugs in the year to early 2025, and the large majority pay privately through online pharmacies. NHS access remains a small share and is growing slowly. UK private Mounjaro now costs roughly £86 to £340 a month after Lilly's September 2025 list-price rise, so Tidemark's £3.99/month is about 1 to 5% of a user's monthly drug bill (£19.99/year is about £1.67/month). In the US about 12% of adults (late 2025) say they are currently taking a GLP-1.

### Cited Findings
**UK user numbers**
- UCL study (BMC Medicine, published January 2026), using the Smoking Toolkit Study survey of 5,260 adults in Jan to Mar 2025: about 1.6 million adults in England, Wales and Scotland used weight-loss drugs in the past year; a further 3.3 million said they would consider using them in the coming year; four in five of those using them solely for weight loss took Mounjaro; about 15% used a product not licensed for weight loss; the survey did not record BMI. — [UCL news](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2026/jan/16-million-uk-adults-used-weight-loss-drugs-past-year); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1111695); [News-Medical, 7 Jan 2026](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260107/Millions-in-Great-Britain-using-or-seeking-weight-loss-drugs.aspx)
- IQVIA estimate (as reported by a clinic blog, July 2025): more than 2 million people paying privately, about seven times the NHS number; about 290,000 adults on NHS-funded anti-obesity medication in July 2025. Secondary citation of IQVIA, not the primary dataset. — [HeySlim statistics roundup 2026](https://www.heyslim.co.uk/blog/uk-weight-loss-medication-statistics-2026)
- Industry estimate (IBISWorld blog, February 2026): about 1.4 million Britons a month get weight-loss drugs privately through online pharmacies. Weakly sourced. — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/blog/weight-loss-drugs-uk/44/1126/)
- Earlier retailer figure (undated Yahoo/press report, likely 2024): 500,000 adults prescribed Mounjaro or Wegovy, about 95% sourcing privately online. — [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/weight-loss-jabs-pharmacies-nhs-203651408.html)
- London Assembly (March 2026): "there is no definitive data" on private access; only about 3,000 people in London were getting the drugs on the NHS as of November 2025. — [London Assembly](https://www.london.gov.uk/who-we-are/what-london-assembly-does/london-assembly-current-investigations/weight-loss-jabs-london)
- NHS tirzepatide rollout: target of about 220,000 patients over the first three years, which the Health Foundation and the Commons Health and Social Care Committee put at about 6% of NICE-eligible people. GP prescribing under the new contract from April 2026 is voluntary for practices; cohort 2 starts 23 June 2026 (sources disagree on the BMI threshold). — [BritBrief on Commons committee](https://britbrief.co.uk/health/nhs/mps-blast-nhs-mounjaro-rollout-as-unambitious.html); [Oxford Online Pharmacy, 2026](https://www.oxfordonlinepharmacy.co.uk/blog/can-you-get-mounjaro-on-the-nhs); [Slinic NHS criteria 2026](https://slinic.co.uk/treatment-guide-mounjaro-nhs-2026/)
- An NHS pilot will offer free jabs to more than 100,000 people through gyms, pharmacies, smartphones and online assessments. — [LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/nhs-free-weight-loss-jabs-5HjdcDr_2/)
- Poll (January 2026): one in 10 people would buy weight-loss jabs on social media. — [ITV, 2 Jan 2026](https://www.itv.com/news/2026-01-02/one-in-10-people-would-buy-weight-loss-jabs-on-social-media-poll-finds)

**UK price changes**
- Lilly raised the UK list price of Mounjaro from 1 September 2025: £133 for 2.5mg and £330 for 12.5mg and 15mg, a 170% rise at the top dose (previously about £122). NHS patients are not affected. — [Patient.info](https://patient.info/features/news/mounjaro-price-rise); [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/eli-lilly-mounjaro-uk-price-increase-set-soar-170private-patients-facing-330-monthly-costs-1741172)
- Before the rise, pharmacies charged roughly £92 to £122 a month. — [Heart](https://www.heart.co.uk/news/mounjaro-price-increase-juniper-medexpress-asda/)
- Lilly paused new pharmacy orders ahead of the rise; Pharmacy2U froze prices "until further notice" amid reports of stockpiling. — [Pharmaceutical Journal](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/news/uks-biggest-online-pharmacy-freezes-mounjaro-price-amid-reports-of-stockpiling); [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/drugmaker-pauses-uk-sales-weight-174150562.html)
- Current private Mounjaro prices (comparison sites, mid to late 2026): lowest from £85.90/month for 2.5mg (84 pharmacies checked 22 Nov 2026); Asda about £149 (2.5mg) to £299 (15mg); Boots about £179 to £339; Superdrug about £176 to £334 (Aug 2026). Delivery adds £3.50 to £8.95 an order. — [Monj comparison, Nov 2026](https://monj.co.uk/mounjaro-price-comparison-by-monj/); [Medino, Nov 2026](https://www.medino.com/article/mounjaro-price-comparison); [CompareMeds 2026](https://www.comparemeds.uk/articles/cheapest-mounjaro-uk-2026)
- Wegovy injection, UK private (one provider's survey, May 2026): £80 to £150 for 0.25mg, £219 to £299 for 2.4mg, £269 to £349 for the 7.2mg top dose. Wegovy pill got MHRA authorisation on 11 June 2026; no confirmed UK list price (one projection £179 to £229/month). — [Medino Wegovy 2026](https://www.medino.com/article/wegovy-cost-per-month-uk-2026-guide); [Second Nature](https://www.secondnature.io/guides/weight-loss-medications/wegovy-pill-cost-uk)
- US: Novo Nordisk announced a cut in the US Wegovy monthly list price from about $1,350 to $675 (injection and pill) from 1 January 2027; no equivalent UK cut found. — [Medino / search summary of Novo announcement](https://www.medino.com/article/wegovy-cost-per-month-uk-2026-guide)

**US**
- KFF poll (fielded 27 Oct to 2 Nov 2025, n=1,350, published 14 Nov 2025): 12% of US adults currently taking a GLP-1 (for weight loss or a condition), up from 6% in May 2024; 18% have ever taken one; 15% of women vs 9% of men; 22% of 50 to 64 year olds; 56% of users say it was difficult to afford. — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/poll-1-in-8-adults-say-they-are-currently-taking-a-glp-1-drug-for-weight-loss-diabetes-or-another-condition-even-as-half-say-the-drugs-are-difficult-to-afford/); [KFF May 2024](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/kff-health-tracking-poll-may-2024-the-publics-use-and-views-of-glp-1-drugs/)

### Inferences
- The UK paying audience for Tidemark is the private self-pay group (on the order of 1.5 to 2 million people in 2025), mostly on Mounjaro. These people already pay £90 to £340 a month out of pocket. NHS patients are a small, slow-growing, more supervised group.
- Relative cost: £3.99 is 4.6% of the cheapest £85.90 starter dose, about 2.2% of a £179 Boots 2.5mg month and about 1.2% of a £339 top-dose month. The annual plan works out at about £1.67 a month, under 2% of even the cheapest month. Framing the app fee as a small share of the drug bill ("protect your £250-a-month investment") is defensible.
- The September 2025 price shock made private users very cost-sensitive. Behaviours such as switching pharmacy, stockpiling, staying on lower doses and stretching pens grew because of it (see section 2). Switching pharmacy is a strong reason to want a portable, pharmacy-independent record.
- US: 12% of roughly 260 million adults would be on the order of 30 million people (my rough calculation, not a KFF figure). US GLP-1 tracker apps price above Tidemark (see section 4).

### Gaps
- No official UK count of private users; IQVIA primary data was not accessible. The UCL figure is a survey estimate for Great Britain, not the whole UK.
- No consolidated, authoritative post-rise price list; prices change monthly.
- Exact NHS cohort 2 BMI threshold is unresolved between sources.
- Share of UK users on iPhone is not established (UK iOS share is usually reported at roughly half of smartphones, but I did not source a 2026 figure).

## 2. What GLP-1 users track and worry about

### Takeaway
Gut side effects and muscle loss are the top worries, protein intake is commonly too low, and most users stop within a year and then regain weight. UK users have their own concerns: the multi-dose Mounjaro KwikPen ("golden dose" and click-counting), pharmacy weight-verification rules, price-driven dose stretching and counterfeit pens.

### Cited Findings
- Survey of 1,000 US adults on GLP-1s (Veru, 2024; company-sponsored): more than a third worried about losing lean muscle, second only to gut side effects; 25% reported nausea, constipation or diarrhoea. — [Patient Care Online](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/muscle-loss-during-glp-1-therapy-top-concern-for-patients-using-the-antiobesity-medications)
- Small study (69 GLP-1 users, 3-day food records, Frontiers in Nutrition 2025): protein intake was within range as a share of calories but well below needs per kg of body weight; a retailer write-up puts it at 77g/day eaten vs about 120g needed. Protein targets on GLP-1s are extrapolated, not trial-tested. — [Frontiers in Nutrition 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1566498/full); [GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/classes/glp-1-agonists/glp-1-protein)
- Discontinuation: a JAMA Network Open study of more than 125,000 adults (2025) found 65% of people without diabetes stopped their GLP-1 within a year (47% with type 2 diabetes). — [Fox News summary](https://www.foxnews.com/health/study-reveals-how-rapidly-weight-comes-back-after-quitting-obesity-medications); [Amgen summary (manufacturer material)](https://www.amgen.com/obesity/prescription-gap)
- Regain: an Oxford-led BMJ meta-analysis (January 2026, 37 studies) found average regain of 0.4kg/month after stopping, with weight and cardiometabolic markers projected back to baseline in under two years; only eight studies covered newer drugs, with at most 12 months follow-up. — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/what-happens-after-you-quit-weight-loss-drugs-a-new-study-offers-some-clues/); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1111469)
- Tapering and weaning off are now a mainstream topic (Harvard Health). — [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/staying-healthy/weaning-off-a-glp-1-tips-for-the-transition)
- UK pen format: the MHRA approved a four-dose Mounjaro KwikPen (multi-dose pen). — [MHRA press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/four-dose-mounjaro-kwikpen-approved-by-mhra-for-diabetes-and-weight-management)
- "Golden dose" (drawing out the leftover priming liquid for a fifth dose) and microdosing by counting pen clicks spread on TikTok in 2025. Pharmacies and clinicians warn that the leftover liquid is there to prime the pen, that click-counting is unreliable and unsafe, and that microdosing is not licensed in the UK. — [Asda Online Doctor on 5th dose](https://onlinedoctor.asda.com/uk/mounjaro-5th-dose.html); [Oviva](https://oviva.com/uk/en/microdosing-mounjaro/); [Medino](https://www.medino.com/faq/why-microdosing-mounjaro-is-not-licensed); [LADbible, June 2025](https://www.ladbible.com/news/health/mounjaro-golden-dose-method-doctors-warning-side-effects-538036-20250605)
- A US safety source says click-counting is impossible on Mounjaro/Zepbound because US pens are fixed single-dose. This applies to US autoinjectors, not the UK multi-dose KwikPen. — [ConsumerMedSafety](https://www.consumermedsafety.org/safety-articles/errors-possible-when-microdosing-counting-clicks-to-dose-weight-loss-medicines)
- Counterfeits: an MHRA Drug Safety Update (February 2026) covers falsified Mounjaro KwikPen 15mg pens (batch D873576) supplied through a UK online pharmacy; patients should check batch numbers. — [MHRA Drug Safety Update](https://www.gov.uk/drug-safety-update/falsified-mounjaro-kwikpen-15mg-pre-filled-pens)
- Pharmacy rules (GPhC guidance, 4 February 2025): prescribers must independently verify weight, height and/or BMI before supplying, for example by video consultation, in person, from clinical records or by contacting another provider. A questionnaire, phone call, or patient-supplied photo or pre-recorded video alone is not enough. There must be a way to "monitor and verify" progress periodically. From 2026, pharmacies fail inspection if they do not independently verify. — [GPhC FAQs](https://www.pharmacyregulation.org/pharmacies/standards-and-guidance-registered-pharmacies/providing-weight-management-services-faqs); [ITV, 3 Feb 2025](https://www.itv.com/news/2025-02-03/weight-loss-jabs-patients-to-face-more-stringent-checks); [Pharmaceutical Journal](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/news/gphc-inspection-framework-update-adds-guidance-on-supervision-websites-and-weight-loss)
- Reorders in practice (pharmacy sales pages, 2026): MedExpress reviews weight with every order and requires about 5% loss after six months at the top tolerated dose to keep prescribing; Click2Pharmacy and Happy Pharmacy use a reorder questionnaire or clinical update rather than new photos; new patients often need a BMI verification video or a full-length photo plus ID. — [MedExpress](https://www.medexpress.co.uk/clinics/weight-loss/mounjaro); [Click2Pharmacy](https://click2pharmacy.co.uk/product/mounjaro-weight-loss-injections/); [Happy Pharmacy](https://happypharmacy.co.uk/products/mounjaro); [Asda Online Doctor](https://onlinedoctor.asda.com/uk/mounjaro.html)
- Which? found Superdrug, MedExpress, Voy and Zava supplied jabs on false information, which pushes pharmacies toward stricter checks. — [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/online-pharmacies-selling-weight-loss-jabs-inappropriately-adymV3c0LJ6x)

### Inferences
- Recurring needs Tidemark can serve without giving advice: a reliable record of dose, date and site; side-effect logs over time and against the dose; weight trend at each dose step (plateau visibility); reorder-ready weight history (useful when the pharmacy asks for a clinical update, or for the "5% at six months" checkpoint); pen and batch records (counterfeit alerts name batch numbers); and a record that survives switching pharmacy after price rises.
- Three moments when motivation to pay is high: (a) the first weeks and each dose step, when side effects peak; (b) a plateau or stall, especially near a pharmacy's continuation checkpoint; (c) stopping or tapering, given 65% stop within a year and regain of about 0.4kg/month. A "life after the jab" maintenance trend view fits (c) and keeps users subscribed after the medication ends.
- Reorder check-ins are a UK-specific need, but Tidemark cannot replace the pharmacy's independent verification. An app record or PDF is supporting evidence, not verification. Marketing should not imply it satisfies GPhC checks.

### Gaps
- Reddit communities (r/MounjaroUK etc.) could not be searched directly with these tools; no first-hand thread evidence was gathered. Recurring needs above come from press, pharmacy and study sources instead.
- No UK-specific survey of side-effect prevalence or tracking habits found.
- Per-provider reorder rules (Boots, Superdrug, Voy, Juniper, Numan) were not confirmed from primary pages.

## 3. Jobs an app could do, and which are medical-device risky

### Takeaway
Recording, reminding, visualising and exporting the user's own data are low risk. Anything that tells the user what dose to take, when to escalate, how many clicks equal a dose, or interprets symptoms or plateaus in clinical terms risks falling under MHRA software-as-a-medical-device rules and must be avoided.

### Cited Findings
- MHRA: whether software is a medical device depends on its intended purpose, and makers must state what it does, for whom and where; an inadequate definition is treated as a serious failure. — [MHRA: crafting an intended purpose](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/crafting-an-intended-purpose-in-the-context-of-software-as-a-medical-device-samd/crafting-an-intended-purpose-in-the-context-of-software-as-a-medical-device-samd); [MHRA software and AI as a medical device](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/software-and-artificial-intelligence-ai-as-a-medical-device/software-and-artificial-intelligence-ai-as-a-medical-device)
- MHRA clinical calculators appendix: calculations users cannot easily verify (for example hidden lookup tables) are likely to be devices; simple, verifiable formulas may be devices without needing UKCA marking. — [MHRA Appendix 2: clinical calculators](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/1103567/Appendix_2_-_clinical_calculators.pdf)
- MHRA stand-alone software and apps guidance (being updated under the Software and AI as a Medical Device change programme). — [MHRA apps guidance PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6abf6c8c943e890cb55e5e52/Medical_device_stand-alone_software_including_apps__including_IVDMDs.pdf); [Change programme roadmap](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/software-and-ai-as-a-medical-device-change-programme/software-and-ai-as-a-medical-device-change-programme-roadmap)
- A search summary of the MHRA GB flow chart guidance (August 2025) said software that "influences dose or treatment choice is unlikely to be a device". This reads like a summariser error and goes against the rest of MHRA guidance, so it should not be relied on until the primary PDF is checked. — [MHRA GB flow chart guidance v2](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68a87c553a052c9c504c8db0/GB_Flow_Chart_Accompanying_Guidance_v2.pdf)
- Click-counting and "golden dose" are unlicensed and described as unsafe by UK pharmacies and clinicians (see section 2). — [Oviva](https://oviva.com/uk/en/microdosing-mounjaro/); [Asda Online Doctor](https://onlinedoctor.asda.com/uk/microdosing-mounjaro.html)

### Inferences
Likely safe (logging and display; keep the intended purpose as "personal record-keeping and sharing with your clinician"):
- Dose history with the weight trend at each dose; injection-site log and a neutral rotation reminder of the last site used; side-effect diary with frequency and severity charts against the date and the user's recorded dose; dose-day reminders at the user's own schedule; pen and supply tracking (doses left in a pen as entered by the user, batch number, expiry, refill date); a pharmacy-reorder and consultation prep checklist with a weight summary; a PDF report for the prescriber; a maintenance or post-stop trend view.

Risky, avoid or word with great care:
- Suggesting or confirming a dose, titration step, or when to escalate, reduce or stop.
- Any click-to-mg converter, "extra dose" or microdosing calculator, or counting doses beyond the pen's labelled count. A "doses left" counter should be capped at the label (for example 4) and should never suggest a fifth dose.
- Plateau "explanations" that attribute a stall to dose or recommend a change; keep to descriptive statements ("your 4-week average has changed by X") and suggest discussing with the prescriber.
- Side-effect triage (for example "this is normal" or "seek help") beyond generic, static safety signposting such as NHS 111 and the MHRA Yellow Card scheme.
- Protein or calorie targets computed from body weight, and muscle-loss risk scores (an unverifiable calculation that drives treatment-like decisions).
- Claiming the app or PDF satisfies GPhC weight verification.

### Gaps
- No MHRA ruling found on GLP-1 tracker apps specifically. Legal review of Tidemark's intended-purpose statement is advisable.

## 4. Willingness to pay and the effect of privacy / no-account positioning

### Takeaway
Direct evidence on what GLP-1 users will pay for a companion app is thin, but the leading US tracker (Shotsy) charges well above Tidemark's price and reportedly earns substantial revenue. Only 9% of US users have a GLP-1-specific app, so there is room to grow. Privacy improves trust and adoption, but older evidence suggests people will not pay more for privacy alone, so privacy should be framed as a reason to trust, with the paid features as the reason to pay.

### Cited Findings
- Shotsy (GLP-1 tracker, iOS): 4.8 stars from about 20,000 US ratings; free download with in-app subscriptions. Adapty estimates 30,000+ downloads a month and about $300,000 monthly revenue (third-party estimate); monthly plans $9.99 to $14.99 and yearly $39.99 to $59.99 (regional variation). — [Adapty paywall library](https://adapty.io/paywall-library/shotsy-glp-1-tracker/); [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6499510249)
- PwC GLP-1 consumer survey (2026, US): only 9% of current users have a GLP-1-specific app; nearly a quarter are "figuring it out on their own"; only 23% feel well supported by wellness platforms. (PwC page returned 403; figures from search and eMarketer summaries.) — [PwC GLP-1 consumer trends 2026](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/industries/consumer-markets/library/glp-1-consumer-trends.html); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/weight-loss-drug-users-piecing-together-their-own-support-systems-manage-treatment)
- Noom bundles its GLP-1 Companion free with Noom plans and reports (company data) that the most frequent users had more weight loss and stayed on medication longer. — [Noom GLP-1 Companion](https://www.noom.com/med/glp1-companion/)
- JMIR 2026 retrospective study of the Mosh app: adding a behavioural app to GLP-1 treatment was associated with a higher chance of clinically significant weight loss; differences in weight trajectory were modest. — [JMIR 2026](https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e100728)
- KFF (Nov 2025): 56% of US users find the drugs hard to afford, which limits extra spend. — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/poll-1-in-8-adults-say-they-are-currently-taking-a-glp-1-drug-for-weight-loss-diabetes-or-another-condition-even-as-half-say-the-drugs-are-difficult-to-afford/)
- Data & Policy (Cambridge, 2025; more than 2,000 respondents in London and Hong Kong): willingness to use health apps rises with data control and comfort sharing data; people share more when they have more control; the 23andMe bankruptcy raised fears about consumer data being sold. — [Cambridge Core blog, Oct 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/blog/2025/10/02/apps-are-making-personalised-health-advice-possible-but-what-do-people-think-about-their-privacy-risks); [UCL Discovery PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10218496/1/behavioural-perspectives-on-personal-health-data-sharing-and-app-design-an-international-survey-study.pdf)
- Vignette study (JMIR mHealth 2020; Spain, Germany, Netherlands): data protection made people more likely to download a health app, but they were not willing to pay more for it. — [JMIR mHealth 2020](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2020/6/e17272/XML)
- arXiv 2023: privacy concern had only a weak effect on mHealth adoption; digital literacy was the strongest factor. — [arXiv 2305.05962](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2305.05962)

### Inferences
- Tidemark's £3.99/£19.99 sits well below Shotsy's US price anchors ($9.99 to $14.99/month, $39.99 to $59.99/year). This leaves room to test higher UK prices, or to present the price as clearly cheap compared with the drug bill.
- Privacy and no account is a trust and download driver (stigma around jabs, the 15% unlicensed use, fear of data sale), not a price premium. The paywall should sell outcomes such as "ready for your pharmacy check-in", "show your prescriber", "see what each dose did" and "keep it off after you stop".
- Best paywall moments: the first dose-step change, logging a second or third side effect, a 2 to 3 week stall, the day before a reorder or consultation (PDF export), and logging a stop or taper.

### Gaps
- No UK study of willingness to pay for a GLP-1 app; no 2025 to 2026 study directly measuring payment for privacy in health apps.
- Shotsy's revenue and download numbers are third-party estimates. No UK-specific competitor revenue data found.
