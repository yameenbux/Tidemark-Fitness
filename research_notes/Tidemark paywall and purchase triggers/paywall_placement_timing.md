# Paywall placement and timing: trigger points and what each does to conversion (for Tidemark, freemium iPhone weight tracker)

Context: Tidemark currently shows Plus once right after onboarding, contextual teasers when a Plus feature is tapped, and a locked preview of the user's own medication-dose data. Research date: November 2026. Nearly all quantitative evidence below comes from subscription-infrastructure vendors (Adapty, RevenueCat, Superwall) that sell paywall tooling; treat it as directional, not independent.

## 1. What share of trials and purchases come from the onboarding paywall vs later placements (day 0 vs later), and how does Health & Fitness differ?

### Takeaway
Day 0 dominates trial starts (about 82% in Health & Fitness, 90% across apps in Adapty's data), and an onboarding paywall converts roughly 1.5 to 4 times better per view than later paywalls. But for freemium apps a large tail of revenue arrives later: about half of all conversions happen after day 0, 23% of freemium conversions come after week 6, and later paywalls (70% of all paywall views) bring in 38% of subscription revenue. So the onboarding paywall should stay, and the later placements are where a freemium app like Tidemark leaves money on the table.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026 (115,000+ apps): Health & Fitness has 82.1% of trial starts on day 0 (Business is highest at 89.9%, Productivity lowest at 78%). Days 1 to 3 capture roughly 5 to 12% of the remaining starts. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)
- Same report: overall 50.6% of conversions happen on day 0, and 19.2% come in week 6 or later. For both freemium and hard-paywall apps, about one third of conversions happen on day 0 and over 60% within the first week. For freemium, 23% of conversions come after 6+ weeks. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)
- Same report, hard paywall vs freemium: median download-to-paid by day 35 is 10.7% for hard paywalls vs 2.1% for freemium; revenue per install at day 14 is $2.32 vs $0.27. Year-one retention is nearly identical for the two models. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)
- Health & Fitness benchmarks (RevenueCat 2026): median download-to-paid (D35) 2.9%, top quartile above 6.2%; median download-to-trial 6.9%, top performers above 23%; median trial-to-paid 37.7%, top quartile above 51.4%; 59% of apps use a mixed (some plans with trial, some without) strategy; 5 to 9 day trials most common (54%); median year-1 realised LTV per payer $35.64. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)
- Earlier report (2025): 82% of trial starts occur the same day the app is installed. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025), also cited in [RevenueCat, "Context wins"](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/contextual-paywall-targeting.md)
- Adapty State of Paywalls 2026 (78,000+ paywalls): onboarding paywalls convert 0.85% of views to paid vs 0.23% for later paywalls ("almost 4x"), and earn $170 vs $40 per 1,000 views. — [Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)
- Same report: 7 in 10 paywall views happen after the first session, and those views generate 38% of subscription revenue; 53% of subscription revenue starts in the first session. 93% of first purchases happen on day 0; 60% of buyers buy on their first paywall view and 15% on the second. — [Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)
- Same report, Health & Fitness: only 40% of buyers purchase on the first paywall view (lower than the 60% all-app figure), H&F has the highest trial-to-paid in every region (19.8% MEA to 33.3% US) but ranks last for trial start rate in every non-US region; 62% of H&F revenue comes from annual plans. — [Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)
- Adapty State of In-App Subscriptions 2026 (16,000+ apps, $3B revenue): with a trial, onboarding paywalls convert at 1.35% vs 0.89% in-app; without a trial 0.82% vs 0.76%. 90% of trial starts and 44.5% of all purchases happen on day 0; category range for day-0 trial starts is 71 to 95%. — [Adapty, high-performing paywall 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/high-performing-paywall-2026/)
- Same dataset: Health & Fitness is the only category where annual's share is still growing (51% to 61% of plans, 2023 to 2025); trials raise LTV in Health & Fitness (they reduce it in Productivity, Lifestyle, Entertainment). — [Adapty, high-performing paywall 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/high-performing-paywall-2026/)
- Note the conflict in the data: RevenueCat says about half of conversions are on day 0, while Adapty says 93% of *first purchases* and 44.5% of *all purchases* are on day 0. The definitions differ (first purchase vs conversion including trial-to-paid, which happens after the trial ends), so they are not directly comparable. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/); [Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)

### Inferences
- For Tidemark, the onboarding paywall is the single most valuable placement and should not be removed; but "show once" leaves the 70% of paywall views (and roughly 38% of revenue) that typically happen later largely unserved.
- Health & Fitness buyers are less likely than average to buy on first view (40% vs 60%), which argues for a second and third exposure rather than a single onboarding shot.
- Because freemium apps get almost a quarter of conversions after week 6, long-tail triggers (milestones, re-shows, win-back) matter more for Tidemark than for a hard-paywall app.

### Gaps
- No source broke out onboarding vs later paywall share specifically for Health & Fitness freemium apps; the H&F figures are category-wide (hard and freemium mixed).
- No weight-tracking-specific data (e.g. Lose It, MyFitnessPal, Noom) on placement split was found.

## 2. Evidence on contextual or feature-gated paywalls, milestone or "moment of delight" triggers, usage-count triggers, and re-showing the paywall after N sessions or days

### Takeaway
Practitioner consensus favours (a) feature-gated or contextual paywalls shown at the moment of intent, (b) a paywall right after the "aha" moment, and (c) repeated exposure (Superwall's Jake Mor says the winning combination in most apps is onboarding + every app open + before any locked feature). Evidence is mostly vendor case studies with large but unaudited lifts; there is no independent controlled study isolating milestone timing.

### Cited Findings
- Superwall-published case study: JourneyStamp replaced one generic paywall with context-specific paywalls tied to each user's journey and reports a 50% conversion lift to a 7.3% overall conversion rate (vendor case study, sample size and significance not given). — [Superwall, JourneyStamp](https://superwall.com/case-studies/journeystamp)
- RevenueCat (Nov 2023): Greg (plant care) originally triggered its paywall on a usage count (adding a 6th plant); after showing the paywall to all users in onboarding, trial sign-ups rose about 400% and sign-up-to-trial went from 3% to 15%. It also gated water reminders behind the subscription. Rootd moved its paywall earlier in onboarding for a 5x revenue increase. — [RevenueCat, paywall placement](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-placement)
- Same article: Superwall CEO Jake Mor says that on most apps the winning combination is showing the paywall on every app open, before any locked feature, both before and after onboarding (practitioner opinion, no figures). PhotoRoom shows its paywall immediately after the user's first background removal (aha-moment placement). Mor also suggests placing a paywall *before* the aha moment if many sign-ups never reach it. — [RevenueCat, paywall placement](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-placement)
- Superwall's recommended high-converting placements (Jul 2024): first touch per session (`touches_began`), transaction abandon, paywall decline, and after a survey response (can be tailored to the answer). No conversion numbers given. — [Superwall blog](https://superwall.com/blog/show-a-paywall-during-these-three-high-converting-app-experiences)
- RevenueCat (Dec 2025, Felix Krause): argues for showing paywalls "at better moments" rather than more often, using context (session depth, motion, time of day). Vendor case studies (Context SDK) cited: Blackbox puzzle game got a 50% revenue increase in peak periods by changing *when* paywalls appeared; Wizz cut prompt volume by about 50% and timed offers to receptive contexts for an 81% lift on the main offer flow and about 20% LTV. Fitness example (hypothetical): full upgrade view when stationary, light nudge only mid-workout. — [RevenueCat, Context wins](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/contextual-paywall-targeting.md)
- Apple's own subscription guidance recommends that freemium and metered apps show "contextually relevant prompts", e.g. when someone nears a free limit, and test messaging variants. — [Apple, Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)
- Adapty: 60% of buyers buy on the first paywall view and 15% on the second, so a second exposure still captures meaningful conversions. — [Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026 cites a contributor reporting that a reverse trial (full premium access first, then downgrade) lifted freemium conversion from 0.4% to 4.5% (anecdotal, single app). — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)

### Inferences
- Candidate new triggers for Tidemark (to A/B test, not proven): first goal or progress milestone (e.g. first 1 kg / 5% lost, a 7-day logging streak) paired with a Plus feature that extends it (trend projections, deeper insights); a weekly-summary moment; a re-show on app open capped by frequency (e.g. once every N sessions or days); paywall after a "what's your goal" survey answer.
- Milestone moments in a weight app are emotionally double-edged (a plateau or gain is the opposite of delight); triggers should fire only on positive events.
- Session-open re-shows are reported to work in vendor commentary, but the Wizz case suggests fewer, better-timed prompts can beat more prompts; test frequency caps against retention, not just conversion.

### Gaps
- No controlled, independent study isolating a milestone or streak trigger and reporting its lift was found.
- No data on optimal N for "re-show after N sessions or days" was found.
- No weight-loss or Health & Fitness app case study (Noom, MyFitnessPal, Lose It, Headspace, Duolingo) with published placement numbers was found in this pass.

## 3. Personalised paywalls, user's own data behind a lock (blurred previews), and paywall design elements (plan default, annual framing, trial reminder timeline, social proof, single vs multi-page)

### Takeaway
Personalisation is under-used (only 2% of paywall headlines use what the app knows about the user) and top-earning apps personalise far more often; but there is no direct test of a blurred "your own data" preview in a mobile app, and the one rigorous study of blurred previews (news paywalls) found no significant effect. The best-evidenced design levers are: explaining how the trial works (Blinkist-style timeline, +12 to 36% ARPU in Adapty tests, +23% in Blinkist's own report), pre-selecting annual (does not hurt conversion; H&F is annual-heavy), two plan lengths, and multi-page onboarding paywalls.

### Cited Findings
- Personalisation: 1 in 4 apps personalise paywalls; among mid-size apps, 69% of the top 10% by revenue per view personalise vs 42% of the rest. Only 15% personalise on user attributes, 6% on acquisition source, and 2% of headlines use what the app knows about the user (correlational, not causal). — [Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)
- Adapty reports that paywalls personalised to Apple Ads keywords lift install-to-trial by 41% and trial-to-paid by 24% (vendor report; personalisation by search keyword, not user data). — [Adapty, Apple Ads personalisation](https://adapty.io/blog/does-paywall-personalization-work-for-apple-ads/)
- Clear (app) got a 50% conversion lift (20% to 30%) by replacing a features carousel with a preview of the user's program (cited via RevenueCat; closest evidence for "show the user their own plan/result"). — [RevenueCat, app value paywall](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/app-value-paywall)
- Blurred previews (news, not apps): a study of millions of 2022 visits to 21 German and Austrian local news sites found that blurred article previews had no statistically significant effect on subscribe clicks. — [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/02/less-is-more-and-discounts-work-a-new-study-looks-at-the-minutiae-of-paywall-strategy/)
- FT reportedly nearly tripled subscription conversion by personalising paywall messaging and offers from past activity (press report of a company claim). — [Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/digital-journalism/ft-says-ai-personalised-paywall-messaging-has-tripled-conversion-rate/)
- Trial explanation: explaining how the trial works lifted ARPU by 12 to 36% in Adapty's tests; 74% of trial paywalls include reassurance near the CTA. 44% of trials are cancelled within 24 hours (32% within the first hour; Europe 55%, US 40%). — [Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)
- Blinkist's "honest paywall" (timeline: access today, reminder on day 5, charged day 7; reminder 2 days before the end) is reported as +23% conversion and 55% fewer complaints, and in one write-up a notification opt-in jump from 6% to 74% (company-reported, no independent audit). — [RevenueCat, how top apps approach paywalls](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/how-top-apps-approach-paywalls.md); [B2B Pricing Insights](https://b2bpricinginsights.substack.com/p/4-min-read-how-blinkists-new-paywall); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tip-increase-trial-signups-by-notifying-users-when-their-trials-are-about-to-end-51d2e789bf)
- Replications are mixed: Slopes saw about +4% trial starts and far fewer day-0 cancellations; another developer reported +23 to 25% trial starts; Flo's growth lead said on Sub Club that the Blinkist-style paywall did not work for Flo. — [Curtis Herbert, Slopes Diaries](https://blog.curtisherbert.com/slopes-diaries-39-risky-business/); [Betterlaunch](https://www.betterlaunch.co/playbooks/mark-kennedy-jeff-bailey-community-4); [RevenueCat Sub Club, Flo](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/mike-charniatsou-flo-sub-club-podcast-2026.md)
- Plan default: 78% of trial paywalls pre-select annual; pre-selecting annual does not hurt conversion; when weekly is pre-selected 90% of buyers choose weekly vs 46% choosing annual when annual is pre-selected. Two plan lengths: 67% higher conversion (headline figure); "more products" won only 53% of A/B tests. — [Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)
- RevenueCat 2026: 2-plan paywalls are most common in Health & Fitness (60%); H&F typical pricing about $9.99/month and $39.94/year. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)
- One-year retention by plan: annual about 44.1%, monthly about 17.0%, weekly about 3.4%. — [RevenueCat, Context wins](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/contextual-paywall-targeting.md)
- Apple requires the billed amount to be the most prominent pricing element; monthly equivalents or savings must be in a subordinate position and size; free trials must show the length and the price after the trial. — [Apple, Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)
- Social proof: used on 27% of paywalls; no lift metric reported. — [Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)
- Single vs multi-page: Superwall claims multi-page onboarding paywalls convert about 37% better than single-page (vendor claim, methodology not seen). — [Superwall, increase conversion rate](https://superwall.com/solutions/increase-conversion-rate)
- Visual and copy-only paywall tests win on LTV only 34.6% of the time (lowest of any test type); plan-count tests win 57.1%. — [Adapty, high-performing paywall 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/high-performing-paywall-2026/)
- Hard vs soft: hard paywalls show 4.7% trial starts vs 3.2% soft, but soft paywalls get more direct purchases (0.75% vs 0.50%); 22% of apps delay the close button (usually 3 to 5 seconds). — [Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)

### Inferences
- Tidemark's locked medication-dose preview is a form of "show the user their own data", closer to Clear's program preview (+50%) than to a generic blur (news study: no effect). Value likely depends on showing a genuinely useful partial result (e.g. a visible trend line or one insight) rather than only a blur. This is untested; A/B test it.
- Health data sensitivity: blurring a user's own medication data as a sales lever may feel coercive; keep the user's raw logged data always accessible (lock analysis or insights, not the data they entered). This also aligns with Apple 3.1.2(a)'s ban on bait-and-switch (inference, not an Apple ruling).
- Given H&F's annual-heavy mix and Adapty's finding that pre-selecting annual does not hurt conversion, a two-plan paywall (annual pre-selected, monthly alternative) with a trial timeline is the best-evidenced default.

### Gaps
- No controlled test of a blurred preview of the user's own health data in an app was found.
- No independent evidence on questionnaire-driven personalised paywalls (e.g. Noom-style) with published lifts was found.

## 4. Win-back and lapsed-trial offers, Apple promotional and win-back offers, price-sensitive offers (discount on paywall close), and what App Review allows

### Takeaway
Second-chance offers (discount on paywall close, transaction abandon) are used by few apps and perform close to the main paywall; vendor data says transaction-abandon paywalls can be a material revenue share. Apple's native win-back offers only target lapsed *paid* subscribers (minimum 1 month paid), so lapsed trials need promotional offers or offer codes instead. App Review allows dismissible paywalls, discounts and retention offers, but bans bait-and-switch, forced notifications/reviews, and unclear pricing.

### Cited Findings
- Second-chance (decline) paywalls: 4% of apps show one immediately after the main one is closed; it converts at 0.88x the main paywall and wins in 45% of apps; typical discount 38%, a quarter offer 50% or more. — [Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)
- Discount depth vs revenue: up to 30% off yields 90% of full-price revenue over six months; 31 to 60% off yields 70%; above 60% off yields 46%. 56% of returning subscribers who take an offer are still paying three months later vs about 1 in 10 new users. — [Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)
- Superwall case study: transaction-abandon paywalls (shown when a user cancels the App Store purchase sheet) accounted for 17% of total revenue; refund rate 3.3% for exposed users vs 6.8% for others (vendor study, reportedly 18 apps, ~500,000 users). — [Superwall, 17% revenue boost](https://superwall.com/blog/17-revenue-boost-with-transaction-abandon-paywalls-a-case-study); [Airbridge summary](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/what-happens-after-user-rejects-paywall)
- Apple offer types: introductory (new subscribers, one per subscription group), promotional (existing or former subscribers, up to 10 per subscription, your own logic), offer codes (including "a save offer in your app to a subscriber who has turned off auto-renew"), and win-back (previous subscribers; shown on the App Store, in-app sheet automatically, in Apple Account subscription settings, or via direct link; streamlined purchasing on by default). — [Apple, Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)
- Apple win-back eligibility: minimum paid duration 1 to 24 months (or 3 to 5 years); time since last subscribed minimum 1 month, maximum 24 months; optional wait between offers 2 to 24 months; payment modes pay-as-you-go, pay up front, free; must run at least 3 days. In-app win-back sheet requires iOS 18; Manage Subscriptions display iOS 14.3+. Max 5 live per storefront per subscription. — [App Store Connect Help, win-back offers](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/set-up-win-back-offers/); [RevenueCat guide](https://revenuecat.com/blog/growth/guide-to-apple-win-back-offers)
- RevenueCat guidance: send a win-back offer immediately after a trial is cancelled (personalised message, limited-time discount or extra trial days); a user who responds to a discount and renews signals price was the issue. — [RevenueCat, win-back examples](https://revenuecat.com/blog/growth/win-back-campaign-examples-ideas)
- App Review 3.1.2(a): subscriptions must give ongoing value, last at least 7 days; users must get what they paid for without extra tasks; apps that "trick users into purchasing a subscription under false pretenses or engage in bait-and-switch and scam practices" are removed. 3.1.2(c): before asking a customer to subscribe, clearly describe what they get for the price. — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- App Review 3.2.2(x): apps must not force users to rate/review or perform store actions to access functionality. 5.1.2(i): apps may not require enabling push notifications, location or tracking to access functionality or receive compensation. — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple: "always make it easy for people to access the system-provided management UI where they can cancel"; offers can complement but not replace it. Required on the sign-up screen: subscription name, duration, content, full renewal price shown clearly and prominently, restore purchases, Terms and Privacy links. — [Apple, Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)
- Hard (non-dismissible) paywalls are permitted in practice: 11% of apps use them. — [Adapty State of Paywalls](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/report/)
- UK: the DMCC Act 2024 subscription contracts regime is now expected to come into force in January 2027 (government confirmation reported by DWF, Oct 2026), earlier than the spring 2027 date in the April 2026 consultation response (itself delayed from autumn 2026). Main protections: pre-contract information, renewal reminders, a 14-day initial cooling-off period plus a new 14-day cooling-off after auto-renewal, and easy exit. Secondary legislation and guidance still awaited. — [DWF, Oct 2026](https://dwfgroup.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/10/dmcca-subscription-contracts-regime-preparing-for-the-new-restrictions); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/uk-subscription-law-shakeup-new-rules-pushed-to-autumn-2026); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/cancel-culture-uk-subscription-contracts-regime-takes-shape); [Brodies](https://brodies.com/insights/commercial-contracts-and-outsourcing/the-dmcca-subscription-contracts-the-timeline-just-got-shorter/)

### Inferences
- Lapsed *trial* users (never paid) do not meet Apple's win-back minimum paid duration, so they need promotional offers (former subscribers) or offer codes triggered by Tidemark's own logic (inference from Apple's eligibility rules; check whether a cancelled trial counts as a "former subscriber" for promotional offers in StoreKit).
- A paywall-decline discount is legitimate if prices and the post-discount renewal price are clear; keep discounts at or below about 30% to protect revenue per Adapty's data, and frequency-cap it so users do not learn to always close.
- UK: a Blinkist-style reminder before trial end would align with the DMCC renewal-reminder direction; however, whether the developer or Apple bears the DMCC duties for App Store in-app subscriptions is not settled in the sources found.

### Gaps
- Exact DMCC reminder timing for free trials and low-cost introductory offers, and how the regime treats App Store billing, were not found (secondary legislation pending).
- No published App Review rejection cases on exit-offer discounts or delayed close buttons were found.

## 5. Notification-driven purchase prompts: allowed or not, and what works

### Takeaway
Allowed on iOS only with explicit, in-app opt-in consent language for promotional push and an in-app way to opt out (guideline 4.5.4); notifications must not carry sensitive personal data, which matters for medication data. Evidence on what works is thin: trial-ending reminders are the best-supported use (Blinkist), while claims about discount pushes are vendor anecdotes.

### Cited Findings
- 4.5.4: push notifications "should not be used for promotions or direct marketing purposes unless customers have explicitly opted in to receive them via consent language displayed in your app's UI, and you provide a method in your app for a user to opt out"; must not send sensitive personal or confidential information. — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.2(i): apps may not require push notifications to be enabled to access functionality. — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple subscription guidance: notifications should be "timely, serve a clear purpose, and deliver meaningful information"; marketing push requires explicit opt-in with consent language and a clear opt-out. — [Apple, Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)
- Blinkist's trial reminder (promised on the paywall, sent 2 days before charge) is reported to have raised notification opt-in from 6% to 74% (company-reported via secondary write-up). — [B2B Pricing Insights](https://b2bpricinginsights.substack.com/p/4-min-read-how-blinkists-new-paywall)
- A RevenueCat-hosted article says push notifications can raise conversion by more than 25% (no study, sample or baseline; anecdotal). — [RevenueCat, lifecycle marketing](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/lifecycle-marketing-campaigns-optimize-revenue)
- Apple win-back offers can be shared via direct link through your own channels (e.g. email), which a push could carry for opted-in users. — [Apple, Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)

### Inferences
- For Tidemark: separate the transactional trial-ending reminder (service message) from promotional offers (requires a distinct marketing opt-in). Never put dose or weight values in push copy.
- A push that deep-links to a milestone moment (e.g. weekly summary) and lets the in-app paywall do the selling is likely safer than a push that is itself an offer.

### Gaps
- No controlled data found on conversion from promotional push offers in Health & Fitness apps.
- Whether a trial-ending reminder counts as "promotional" under 4.5.4 is not stated by Apple.
